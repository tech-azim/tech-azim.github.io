01 / 04 — 2023

IFG Life — insurance mobile experience

Frontend developer · 12 months · 3‑team agile

A customer mobile app for one of Indonesia's largest life-insurance providers, used by policyholders to view coverage, file claims, and manage premiums.

React NativeNext.jsTypeScriptTailwindRedux
life.id ↗
Problem

The app took ~2 seconds to reach interactive, mostly on the policy summary screen — the very thing 80% of sessions opened to. In rural districts, half the sessions failed before that screen finished loading at all.

Process
  • Profiled the render tree on three real devices in three coverage tiers — not just the office Wi-Fi.
  • Mapped which UI was actually visible in the first 1.5s of a session, and cut everything else from the critical path.
  • Co-designed an offline cache contract with backend so policy data survived dropped sessions.
Key decisions
  • Spent the animation budget on the policy summary, not the splash screen.
  • Cached policy and claim payloads locally with a freshness window — readers shouldn't pay the latency cost twice.
  • Standardized our component library so juniors stopped reinventing buttons.
Outcome
Sub-1 second policy load (from ~2s). UI bug reports down ~30% in the quarter after the component cleanup. App store rating held above 4.5.
02 / 04 — 2026

Learnova — certification & training platform

Design + frontend lead · solo build · freelance

End-to-end platform for an Indonesian professional certification body. Replaced a stack of spreadsheets and PDFs with one tool that assessors, participants, and admins all share.

Next.jsshadcn/uiGo (Gin)PostgreSQLFigma
learnovaconsultant.id ↗
Problem

Cohorts of 200+ candidates were tracked in shared spreadsheets. Assessors had three browser tabs open per session. Every certificate was issued by hand, which made verification a phone call.

Process
  • Mapped three journeys in parallel: participant, assessor, admin. They wanted very different things from the same data.
  • Prototyped two assessor flows in Figma — a multi-page form vs. a single scoring console — and tested both with the actual assessor team.
  • Designed a verifiable certificate format with a public lookup URL, so HR teams could check authenticity without calling.
Key decisions
  • Single-page assessor console won. Two pages was one too many.
  • Made participant registration a strictly linear flow — no save-and-resume — because every drop-off was a lost candidate.
  • Used shadcn/ui primitives so the admin console moved at the same speed as the public site.
Outcome
Cohort administration is now one tool, not five. Certificates verify themselves. Live as the official platform for the certification body.
03 / 04 — 2024

Dimedika — EHR for independent clinics

Design + fullstack · freelance

A small electronic health record for solo doctors and small clinics. Designed around the real unit of work — a 15‑minute consultation — not the IT department's wishlist.

Next.jsshadcn/uiNode.jsMongoDB
dimedika.id ↗
Problem

Independent clinics couldn't afford hospital-grade EHRs and didn't need their feature lists. Their stack was a paper register and WhatsApp. Notes got lost between rooms.

Process
  • Shadowed two clinics for a half-day each. Watched what doctors actually do in the consultation room.
  • Wrote down every action that happened in a 15-minute visit, then designed the home screen around that flow.
  • Tested the prototype on a clinic-issued tablet — desktop assumptions broke immediately.
Key decisions
  • Calendar-first, not patient-list-first. The real question is "who's next?", not "who do I have on file?"
  • Larger touch targets and dense cards optimized for tablet; desktop got the same layout, not a separate one.
  • Versioned every record edit so corrections are traceable without scaring users with a "who changed this" dialog.
  • Cut billing and inventory from v1 — clinics already had something for that.
Outcome
In production with paying independent clinics. Patient handoff between consultation rooms went from a paper-shuffle to a tap.
04 / 04 — 2026

Kopi Kita — AI CRM for small coffee shops

Design + build · solo · personal project

A side project. A CRM where the shop owner just chats with their customer data — no filter trees, no segment builder. Live demo and full source.

Next.js 15SupabasepgvectorGroq LLMAnt Design
live demo ↗
Problem

Independent cafés want to keep regulars but can't justify a real CRM seat — and the ones that exist are designed for marketing teams, not for someone running a counter alone.

Process
  • Sketched what a "one-person CRM" looks like — single screen, no nav.
  • Picked Supabase + pgvector specifically because the whole stack had to be runnable and deployable solo.
  • Iterated the LLM prompt for weekly promo themes until it stopped suggesting "a refreshing summer drink" in November.
Key decisions
  • Chat-first interface for owners. Semantic search over a customer table beats a filter sidebar nobody uses.
  • RAG with vector search + a keyword fallback, so the empty-query state still feels alive.
  • Weekly promo drafts come pre-written — the owner edits, doesn't compose.
Outcome
Public demo and full source — a useful talking point for AI-product roles, and a working answer to "show me applied LLM work."

About

The short version

Five years building product interfaces in industries where the software has to work — insurance, healthcare, government, education. I design in Figma and ship in React.

I started as a fullstack engineer and drifted toward the design side because most of the bugs I was fixing weren't bugs — they were UI decisions made too fast. I now sit between the two: I'll prototype an interaction in Figma, then implement it myself, which means design decisions don't survive only on slides.

I work best in small teams that ship often. I've led frontend tracks inside three-team sprints, mentored juniors, and run solo client builds end-to-end. I'm comfortable owning the whole frontend, and I'm equally comfortable working inside a design system someone else owns.

Outside work I publish small open-source utilities, collect mechanical keyboards I don't strictly need, and work out of Bandung.
